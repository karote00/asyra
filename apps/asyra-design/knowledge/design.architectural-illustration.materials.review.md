# Inspecting a material decision

Use this advice when material appearance is uncertain. It does not prescribe a
mandatory sample, extra stage or tool attestation. Choose the observations that
answer the unresolved question: overall light direction, local reflection shape,
contrast against adjacent surfaces or continuity across a join. Material accuracy
and missing geometric detail are separate questions.

Use the actual created IDs from acknowledged receipts. A group capture excludes
nearby siblings; choose their common ancestor when the decision involves both.
An overview shows the entire target at a bounded scale. Native detail requires a
small enough target or a target-local region within the published capture bound.
Reference pixels, world coordinates and target-local coordinates are distinct.

If construction and the needed view are already decided, submit them together
through execute_design, binding the created root into the registered
inspect_drawing operation. Select that capture's result for image delivery.
Save useful stage parameters or results with retain when they may help later;
saving alone does not display an image.

Inspect the delivered pixels yourself. If the selected crop misses the relevant
surface, obtain the correct view without rebuilding it. When the material needs
correction, preserve unrelated geometry and adjust the appropriate existing values
or material mapping. If a decision is already adequate, continue; there is no
required verdict registration, evidence binding or fixed image count.

Explicit numerical or pixel comparisons may help when their inputs and thresholds
are known. They answer their declared mechanical question, not whether the result
looks natural. Review the final result against the user request and state unresolved
limitations. Capture failure and unknown write settlement require boundary recovery;
a missing quality approval does not. Never replay a completed write to recover an
image or a lost description.
