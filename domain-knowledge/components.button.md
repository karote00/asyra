# Buttons with clear action and priority

## What

A button presents a specific action with a readable label and a clear visual boundary. Primary, secondary and quiet variants communicate relative emphasis.

## Why

Several equally prominent buttons can compete for attention. Vague labels make the consequence of activation difficult to predict.

## How

Start with a concise verb phrase that matches the action. Size padding around the real label; inspect wrapping and icon alignment. Give the main action an emphasis appropriate to its context and distinguish alternatives through fill, outline or spacing. In a static design, describe proposed interaction states without pretending they are implemented.

## Provenance

Project-authored synthesis of common design practice. No external source is quoted and no empirical outcome is asserted. Consumer Apps may specialize this advice for their audience and task.

## Evaluation Guidance

Compare the button with nearby text and competing actions. Check label readability, spacing and the intended target size. Product constraints and user intent may justify a different emphasis or shape; this is advisory design knowledge.
